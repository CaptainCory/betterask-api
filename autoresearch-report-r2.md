## New Patterns Discovered (Beyond Round 1's Three Rules)

### Pattern 4: The Parental Mirror
**Questions about the parent-child break point consistently score 9.**

Examples: "When did you stop trying to impress your parents?" / "What did your parents fight about that you now understand?" / "What did you inherit from your parents that you had to uninstall yourself?"

**Why it works:** The parent-child relationship is the universal identity forge. Every human has one. Questions that ask about the MOMENT OF SEPARATION — when you stopped being their mirror and started being yourself — touch the deepest identity layer. This is different from generic "tell me about your family" because it asks about the BREAK, not the bond.

**Template:** "What did you [agency verb] about/from your parents that [created independence]?"

### Pattern 5: Secret Goodness
**Questions about hidden acts of kindness or commitment that nobody knows about score 9.**

Examples: "What did you sacrifice for someone who never knew?" / "What's a kindness you did that you kept to yourself?" / "What's a promise you made to someone that you never told them about?"

**Why it works:** Different from Hidden Self (Rule 1) in a crucial way — Hidden Self asks "what are you hiding?" which implies shame. Secret Goodness asks "what good did you do that you never advertised?" which implies *character*. People light up answering these because they get to reveal the best version of themselves that nobody sees. It's vulnerability without shame. Pure warmth.

**Template:** "What's a [positive act] you did that [nobody/never marker]?"

### Pattern 6: The Body Question
**Questions about the physical body that touch identity/acceptance score much higher than expected.**

Example: "What part of your body have you made peace with?" scored 9.

**Why it works:** The body is deeply personal but almost never discussed in question games. It's the ultimate "never asked" territory. Everyone has a body story — scars, changes, things they learned to accept. When combined with agency ("made peace with"), body questions reveal identity in a way that's universal, concrete, and intimate.

### Pattern 7: The Fresh Metaphor Verb
**Using a metaphor verb from a different domain (tech, mechanical, botanical) produces surprise.**

Example: "What did you inherit from your parents that you had to uninstall yourself?" — "uninstall" is tech vocabulary applied to psychology. It produces immediate surprise (25% of Cory's rubric) while staying concrete and action-oriented.

**Caution:** Only works when the metaphor feels natural, not forced. "Uninstall" works because we all intuitively understand software installation as something imposed from outside. "Debug your childhood" would be try-hard.

## Rule-Breaking Results

### Rule 3 (Agency > Observation) — CONFIRMED, NO EXCEPTIONS
Five questions were deliberately crafted with observation verbs to test Rule 3:

| Question | Verb Type | Score |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| "What do you see when you look at your hands?" | Pure observation | 5 |
| "What's the first thing you notice about a room?" | Pure observation | 4 |
| "When you watch yourself in old videos, what surprises you?" | Observation + temporal | 6 |
| "What feeling do you get before a big decision?" | Observation + agency-adjacent | 7 |
| "What do you notice about yourself when alone too long?" | Observation + stakes | 6 |

**Verdict:** Observation verbs absolutely cap at 7 even with supporting elements. No 9-scoring question in Round 2 uses an observation verb. Rule 3 is iron.

### Hidden Self Formula — NOT REQUIRED FOR 9 (Important Exception Found)
"When did you stop trying to impress your parents?" scored 9 with NO hidden-self markers (no "nobody/never/secret"). 

**What overrides hidden-self formula:** Touching the AGENCY/INDEPENDENCE moment directly. If the question asks about the exact point where someone took life into their own hands (especially the parental separation moment), it doesn't need "nobody knows" framing. The moment itself is inherently private and rarely discussed.

**Updated rule:** Hidden Self formula is a RELIABLE path to 9, but not the ONLY path. The deeper requirement is: **ask about something that's almost never discussed AND involves a moment of personal agency.**
