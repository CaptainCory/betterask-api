## The 3 Strongest Patterns/Rules Discovered

### Rule 1: The Hidden Self Formula
**Questions that explicitly ask for what's hidden, secret, or never shared consistently score 9.**

Linguistic markers: "nobody knows," "never talk about," "nobody asked," "nobody saw," "you'd never put on a resume."

**Why it works for Cory's taste:** His #1 criterion for a 9 is "asks what's NEVER asked." The Hidden Self formula operationalizes this literally — the question itself says "tell me what nobody asks about." It invites personal stories by definition, because the answer hasn't been rehearsed.

**Template:** "What's [something with stakes] that [nobody/never marker]?"

### Rule 2: The Unexpected Adjective
**Pairing a surprising adjective with a common noun creates instant 9-potential.**

Examples: "quietest rebellion," "loneliest decision," "bravest thing nobody saw," "scar you're glad you have," "strangest thing you're grateful for."

**Why it works:** The adjective creates surprise (25% of Cory's rubric). The noun keeps it concrete (20%). The mismatch forces the answerer to reinterpret a familiar concept — "rebellion" is usually loud, so "quietest" forces a different kind of story. This naturally produces multi-layered answers where the depth depends on how the answerer resolves the tension.

**Template:** "[Unexpected adjective] + [common life noun]?"

### Rule 3: Agency Verbs Over Observation Verbs
**Questions with agency verbs (chose, built, quit, drew, started, kept, broke, staged, forgave) score 8+ almost universally. Questions with observation verbs (notice, see, feel, think about, prefer) consistently score ≤5.**

The calibration data confirms this starkly:
- "What rule do you have for yourself that nobody taught you?" (9) — agency
- "What's the last thing you built with your hands?" (9) — agency  
- "What will you guard against AI changing?" (9) — agency/defense
- "If your phone's screen time was on a billboard..." (3) — observation
- "If AI could replicate your taste in music..." (3) — passive preference

**Why it works:** Cory defines a 9 as "the point where someone took life into their own hands." Agency verbs position the answerer as the protagonist of their own story. Observation verbs position them as a spectator. The difference between "What do you notice about..." and "What did you choose to..." is the difference between a 3 and a 9.

## Updated Generation Prompt (For Consistent 8+ Output)

```
You are generating questions in the style of Cory Stout's END SMALL TALK books.

MANDATORY RULES (every question must satisfy ALL):
1. Contains at least one AGENCY VERB (chose, built, quit, drew, started, kept, broke, 
   staged, forgave, taught yourself, stopped, refused, fought for, created, walked through).
   NEVER use observation verbs (notice, see, prefer, feel about, think about).
2. Is answerable at a bar in 10 seconds OR debatable for 10 minutes.
3. Uses CONCRETE NOUNS, not abstractions (phone call > communication, 
   Amazon > mass-producing, scar > hardship, car > vehicle).
4. Has NO "right" answer — every response reveals personality.

9-SCORE TECHNIQUES (use at least one per question):
- HIDDEN SELF: Include "nobody knows," "never talk about," "nobody saw," "wish they'd asked"
- UNEXPECTED ADJECTIVE: Pair surprising adjective with common noun 
  (quietest rebellion, loneliest decision, bravest thing nobody saw)
- INVERSION: Flip expectations (glad about a fight, grateful for something strange, 
  horrified by old dreams, do the wrong thing again)
- TEMPORAL ANCHORS: "used to... now," "at [age]... completely," "the last time... when"
- SELF-IMPOSED STRUCTURE: Rules, traditions, rituals, lines they drew for themselves
- SPECIFIC PERSON LENS: "What would your [mother/best friend/ex] say about..."

AVOID (these produce ≤5 scores):
- Hypothetical preferences without stakes ("Would you rather...")
- Passive observation exercises ("What does your ___ say about you?")
- Questions requiring database-level memory recall (too broad)
- Classic well-known binaries that have been asked before
- Psychology jargon or therapy-intake energy
- Tech-focused mirror questions (screen time, browser history)
- Cushioned premises that remove real-world tension ("no money worries")
- Questions where one answer is obviously "right"

ARCHETYPE BIAS (by reliability):
- VULNERABILITY DOOR: Highest 9-rate. Lead with these.
- IDENTITY SORT: Second highest. Self-categorization through choices.
- TIME MACHINE: Strong when combined with agency ("stopped," "first realized").
- REFRAME: Good when pairing unexpected setting with important moment.
- SPECIFICITY TRAP: Good when the number reveals stakes (people cut out, 
  friendships that would survive).
- AVOID: Explain-It Test, Absurd Escalation, classic False Binaries — 
  lowest 8+ rate across all cycles.

VOICE: Everyday language. Never academic. Culturally grounded in 2026. 
Respects the answerer. Humor as trojan horse for depth, never the point itself.
```
